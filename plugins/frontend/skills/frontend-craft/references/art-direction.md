# Art direction for public and reading experiences

Modified synthesis of the pinned sources in [sources.md](sources.md); these are contextual design heuristics, not aesthetic requirements.

## Give the brief a visual argument

Choose what the visitor should understand first, what earns their confidence, and what they can do next. Derive a direction from the actual subject: an archive can reveal its catalog structure; a maker can show material and process; a technical product can make its workflow legible. A mood adjective alone does not determine a layout.

Before building a substantial new surface, state a compact direction: intended audience and action, organizing idea, typography roles, palette roles, density, and any meaningful asset or motion choice. Use existing tokens and brand commitments where present. This is a working decision, not a mandatory presentation or approval ceremony. For a small addition, inherit the surrounding direction.

Compose around the content's relationships. A comparison needs alignment; a process needs sequence; an argument needs evidence; an independent collection may need cards. Give the important content more visual weight and let supporting information support it. Equal containers are appropriate when the items are genuinely peers.

## Make deliberate tradeoffs

- **Typography:** Pick families for the subject, available glyphs, legibility, and existing identity. Tune size, weight, line length, and leading together using real copy. One family can carry a complete design. Inspect line breaks on the rendered page; avoid forced breaks that only work at one viewport.
- **Color:** Assign roles for content, surfaces, actions, and states. A quiet or vivid palette can both be distinctive when composition and content justify it. Check actual contrast rather than inferring it from a color name.
- **Spacing:** Use proximity to show relationships and separation to show changes of topic. Repeated spacing should express a system, with intentional exceptions where importance changes.
- **Assets:** Prefer supplied material. Choose an image, illustration, data graphic, or interactive demonstration only when it explains, proves, or expresses something useful. Text-led work is valid. Do not invent testimonials, customer endorsements, or performance claims to fill a composition.
- **Motion:** Use movement to explain change or create a brief-appropriate moment. Keep essential content visible and actions available if motion is reduced or unavailable. Static designs need no motion quota.

## Reading experiences

Organize headings around the reader's questions. Give long pages reliable navigation, useful link text, distinguishable examples and callouts, and an obvious reading order. Start prose around 60–75 characters per line as a heuristic, then tune to the typeface and content; code, tables, and side notes need their own treatment. Preserve access to wide content without forcing the entire page to scroll sideways.

During [rendered review](rendered-review.md), judge specificity by the fit between subject, content, and composition. A familiar font, gradient, neutral palette, or card is not itself a defect. Change the choice when it weakens the brief.
